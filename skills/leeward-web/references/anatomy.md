# Page anatomy by job

A page is built around what the visitor came to *do*, not around a default run of "features, steps,
testimonials, email band". Name the job first (step 2 of the workflow), then build the sections real
businesses in that category are built around. Adapted from Hallmark's anatomy guide
([nutlope/hallmark](https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark), MIT), extended for Leeward's clients.

Every job: the nav carries the job's own action word ("Book", "Reserve", "Enquire", "Tickets"), the
hero shows the thing itself (the boat, the room, the food, the beach), and proof comes in the
category's own form (real reviews with a source, the real menu, the real fleet), never a generic row
of numbers.

## Book a trip (charters, tours, water sports, excursions)

- **Action:** "Book" in the header on every page and a sticky bar on mobile; booking opened from the
  hero and repeated beside every boat or trip, **prefilled** with that trip. Never only at the foot.
- **Sections:** the boats or experiences as large photos with the facts that decide (duration,
  guests, departure times, what's included); where you go (destinations with real photos); how the
  day runs; pickup points; what to bring; questions.
- **Ends:** the booking again, with WhatsApp and phone, then practical details.

## Book a table (restaurants, beach bars, beach clubs)

- **Action:** a reservation picker (party, date, time) or the client's booking tool, opened from the
  nav and the hero; WhatsApp or phone for walk-in questions. Repeated beside the menu.
- **Sections:** the room or the view as a photograph with little or no heading; the menu (switch by
  lunch, dinner and drinks, or rows with prices); events or nights (live music, sunset); how to find
  it; **hours and address as a section of their own**.
- **Ends:** hours, address, map link and the reservation again. A fine room may need almost
  nothing: one photograph, the menu, the booking.

## Stay (villas, boutique hotels, guesthouses)

- **Action:** check availability (dates and guests) or enquire, in the nav and the hero, repeated beside
  each room or villa.
- **Sections:** the place as photographs set big; rooms or villas with the facts (sleeps, beds,
  baths, view, amenities); the location and what's nearby; services (chef, transfers, concierge);
  house rules and policies.
- **Ends:** availability again, then location and contact.

## Visit (shops, galleries, venues, attractions)

- **Action:** today's hours and the address, in the nav or the hero; tickets only if they exist.
- **Sections:** what's on now as the lead picture; what's coming as dated rows; for a shop, the
  shelves (the real products); getting there and parking.
- **Ends:** visit information and the address.

## Attend (events, festivals, regattas, parties)

- **Action:** tickets in the nav from the first screen; the date and the place are the headline.
- **Sections:** the lineup or programme (names set large); the days as a schedule; ticket tiers with
  prices; venue and travel; questions.
- **Ends:** tickets again and the questions.

## Buy (a small shop, a product)

- **Action:** the product with its price and options; a sticky buy bar with the price on mobile.
- **Sections:** the range with prices; the product in use, close; how it's made; delivery, returns and
  guarantee as plain facts; reviews only if real.
- **Ends:** the offer again or new arrivals. A newsletter field belongs in the footer, not as the close.

## Hire (studios, services, trades)

- **Action:** enquire or call, in the nav and after the work.
- **Sections:** the work itself (real projects, big); what they do, in the client's own terms; who
  they are; how a job runs; real clients only if given.
- **Ends:** the enquiry again with contact details.

## How pages end

In the order real sites use them: the hero's action again in a short band; practical details (hours,
address, contacts); more of the catalogue (more trips, more work); a short trust tail (real awards,
press, certifications the client provided). A newsletter sign-up as the closing section is a
template tell unless the business really runs one (events, releases, a recurring offer).

## When content is missing

Never invent it. Build the section as a clearly marked sample the client replaces (dishes typical
of the cuisine, labelled "sample"), and list it in the handoff. A sample never states something a visitor
could take as true: no prices, dates, names, counts or reviews. Practical facts the visitor needs to act
(hours, address) are slots marked for the client to fill, never guessed.
